## 1. What calligraphy is (vs ordinary writing)

| | Everyday Arabic writing | Arabic calligraphy |
|--|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Goal | Be read quickly | Beauty + meaning + discipline |
| Tool | Ballpoint, marker, school pen | **Qalam** (reed pen), special ink & paper |
| Rules | Spelling, joining letters | Proportions, pen angle, rhythm, white space |
| Training | School | Years with a **master**; license (*ijāza*) in classical tradition |
| Output | Notes, messages | Qur’an pages, titles, architecture, art |

Same alphabet. Different **ambition**.

Calligraphy was especially central in Islamic cultures because figurative art was often limited in religious contexts — **the written word itself** became the high art.

## 2. The tool that defines everything: the **qalam**

### 2.1 What it is
- **Qalam** (قلم) = pen, classically a **dried reed** (or bamboo), hollow, cut by hand.
- The tip is **sliced at an angle** and split so ink flows between two tines.
- When you put the flat cut on paper, it makes a **diamond-shaped mark** — the **nuqṭa** (dot) used to measure letters.

### 2.2 Why the cut matters
| Setting | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Nib width** | How thick the boldest stroke can be |
| **Cut angle** | How thick/thin contrast appears as you turn curves |
| **Hold angle** | Common teaching: about **35°** for Naskh/Thuluth; often ~**45°** in beginner guides; **~55°** often cited for Persian/Nastaʿlīq |

**Important:** You generally **do not spin the pen** while writing. Thick and thin come from the **fixed chisel edge** moving through different directions — not from random pressure alone.

### 2.3 Ink and paper (classical craft)
- **Ink:** traditionally soot/lampblack + binder (e.g. gum arabic) + water — rich black, controlled flow.  
- **Likka:** silk fibers in the inkwell that regulate how much ink the pen picks up.  
- **Paper:** often **sized and burnished** (starch, egg white, alum, polish with stone/glass) so the pen glides and edges stay sharp — not ordinary absorbent paper.  
- Calligraphers historically prepared pens, ink, and paper as part of the art.

### 2.4 What this means for our digital app
| Real craft | In **Ink** |
|------------|------------|
| Angled reed tip | `weight` + style pen angle / chisel |
| Wet vs dry ink | `wetness` |
| Steady vs flying hand | `formality` / speed |
| Prepared paper | plain / grid / scroll surfaces |
| Master model to copy | **Show me** + **trace** ghost |

Digital will never fully replace reed-on-paper — but we can **respect the logic** of the craft.

## 3. The measuring system (why letters look “right”)

Codified especially by **Ibn Muqla** (d. 940) and refined by masters like **Ibn al-Bawwāb**:

### Three measures
1. **Nuqṭa** — size of the pen’s diamond dot  
2. **Alif** — height of the vertical (often **5–7 dots** in naskh)  
3. **Circle** — diameter equal to alif height; bowls and curves relate to this circle  

So calligraphy is not “draw anything pretty.” It is **geometry with a living pen**.

### What “good” looks like
- Letters share one **family of sizes**  
- Baseline is steady  
- Thick strokes and thin strokes are **consistent** with one pen angle  
- Space between letters (and inside bowls) feels intentional  

Our **grid** (baseline, mid-line, descender, alif height) is a simplified mashq-style helper for that system.

## 4. The main styles (scripts) you need to know

### 4.1 Early / geometric
**Kufic (كوفي)**  
- Oldest major “formal” look  
- Angular, strong horizontals, architectural  
- Great for titles, ornament, square Kufic mosaics  
- Less about soft brushy flow, more about structure  

### 4.2 The Six Pens (*al-aqlām al-sitta*)
Classical “proportioned scripts” (names vary slightly by school):

| Style | Feel (plain words) | Classic use |
|-------|--------------------|-------------|
| **Naskh** نسخ | Clear, rounded, readable | Books, Qur’an copies, teaching, print |
| **Thuluth** ثلث | Tall, ceremonial, sweeping | Headings, architecture, prestige pieces |
| **Muḥaqqaq** محقّق | Majestic, open, precise | Large Qur’anic lines historically |
| **Rayḥānī** ريحاني | Related to muḥaqqaq, finer/ornate | Decorative Qur’anic hands |
| **Riqāʿ / Ruqʿah** رقعة | Compact, fast, everyday | Notes, modern handwriting, signs |
| **Tawqīʿ** توقيع | Related to riqāʿ, official | Decrees, documents historically |

### 4.3 Later / regional stars
| Style | Feel | Notes |
|-------|------|--------|
| **Dīwānī** ديواني | Dense, ornate, intertwined | Ottoman court elegance |
| **Dīwānī Jalī** | Larger, more decorative diwani | Display |
| **Nastaʿlīq** نستعليق | Hanging, sloping, poetic | Persianate; often steeper pen angle |
| **Maghribī** مغربي | Deep bowls, rounder, western Islamic | North Africa / Andalusī family |

### 4.4 Which style for learning?
Common advice today:
- **Ruqʿah** — fastest start, everyday  
- **Naskh** — best **foundation** (clear rules, readable)  
- **Thuluth** — advanced beauty after basics  
- **Kufic** — geometric creativity  
- **Dīwānī** — decorative once control is strong  

Our app’s style list (naskh, thuluth, ruqah, kufic, diwani, muhaqqaq, maghrebi, nastaliq) matches this map.

## 5. How a calligrapher actually practices

### 5.1 Master → student
- Classical path: copy **models** for years  
- When ready, receive **ijāza** (permission/license) from a master  
- Skill is judged by **hand**, not only by knowing letter names  

### 5.2 Practice sequence (typical modern class)
1. Prepare pen, ink, paper (or good substitutes)  
2. Hold pen at a **fixed angle**  
3. Warm up with **basic strokes** (horizontal, vertical, curves, dots)  
4. Practice **single letters** to proportion  
5. Practice **joins** and short words  
6. Practice full lines, then compositions  
7. Compare constantly to a correct model  

### 5.3 What the hand is doing
- **Whole-arm / controlled wrist** motion more than tiny finger scribbles  
- **Steady angle** of the nib  
- **Rhythm**: not rushing thick strokes  
- **Lifts** only when the style requires (not random)  
- **Looking ahead** to the next letter while finishing the current one  

### 5.4 Composition (beyond single words)
Calligraphy compositions care about:
- Balance of black ink vs empty paper  
- Symmetry or intentional asymmetry  
- Hierarchy (main phrase vs smaller text)  
- Sometimes geometric frames, medallions, mirrored writing  

That’s art direction — after letter skill exists.

## 6. Calligraphy vs “Show me the word” in our product

| User need | Calligraphy truth | App response |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| See the **correct shape** | Model from a school/style | Clean **Show me** of the word (naskh-like type is a valid model for learners) |
| Feel the **pen** | Thick/thin from angled nib | Free-draw with weight / wetness / style angle |
| Sit on a **line** | Baseline discipline | Grid + shared baseline for ghost & demo |
| Learn **order of pen moves** | Body then dots; joins; non-joiners | Future: stroke guides per letter/word (separate from shape demo) |
| Style personality | Naskh ≠ Thuluth ≠ Kufic | Style profiles that change pen defaults + grid kind |

### Honest product boundary
- **True classical calligraphy** = reed, ink, paper, years of proportion training.  
- **Our app** = a **practice studio**: see the word, trace it, freehand it, feel ink controls.  
- Don’t claim “this is a licensed calligraphy master.”  
- **Do** claim: “this respects Arabic direction, baseline, joins, and style personalities.”

## 10. One-page summary

1. Calligraphy is the **art of the measured pen**, not only spelling.  
2. The **reed qalam** (angled tip) creates thick/thin without spinning the pen.  
3. Everything is measured from the **dot**, the **alif**, and a **circle**.  
4. Styles are different **personalities** of the same alphabet (Six Pens + Kufic, Diwani, Nastaʿlīq, Maghribī…).  
5. Training is **copy the model, again and again**.  
6. For our app: respect **baseline, RTL, joins, body→dots**, and style character — and keep Show me **honest** (real letter, solid motion, no fake scribbles).
